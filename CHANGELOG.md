# Changelog

## [Unreleased]

- **D no longer picks a high gear from one spinning wheel.** With the truck at rest and one wheel spinning free, a held gear that went back to D could land in a gear near the top. D now takes the gear it sets off in there, as it already did once it had learned how the wheels turn on the ground.
- **In A a shift no longer throws the wheels into a spin.** The mod makes up for the pull the game takes away near the top of a gear. Until now the figure worked out for the old gear could stand in the new gear for a moment, right as the automatic shifted. In the new gear that was far too much: floored after a low position the wheels could fly to many times their speed, and the automatic then skipped a gear on that reading. Now the make-up is taken away before the gear is set and comes back for the new gear once it is in. The automatic also shifts up one gear at a time when the wheels spin free.
- **A numbered gear held by hand now keeps its wheels to the wheel cap, in every gear.** Each wheel may spin only so far past the top speed of its gear, as in L+. Before, a held gear let the wheels spin up to the engine speed limit. A change at speed takes away no pull in one step. This acts for every player of the mod gearbox.
- **A truck keeps its brake air between drives.** Each truck you own now keeps the air it was left with. Change to another truck and back, and the needle is where you left it. The same holds after a recovery, after a garage visit and after a new start of the game. Nothing leaks while you are away. Before, every truck change started on full tanks. A truck whose id the mod cannot read still starts full.
- **New box "Empty tanks at a start" on the Engine tab, off by default** (`AirEmptyAtStart`). With it on, a truck with air brakes has empty tanks the first time you drive it after the game starts. It stands on its spring brakes until the compressor has filled the tanks to 4.5 bar: about a minute and a half at idle, under half a minute at the red zone. A truck you come back to in the same session is never emptied. The box is for every truck.
- **Truck class is Auto by default: the scales follow the type of the truck.** The Truck class row on the Engine tab has a new first choice, Auto, and it is the default. With Auto the scales go by the type of the truck in the game files and by its weight, as the mod automatic and Air brakes already did. A scout by its type now shows the scales of a scout with no setting made: idle 800 rpm in place of 650, the red zone from 5500 of 7000 in place of 2200 of 3000, the speedometer to 120 km/h in place of 100. A heavy truck shows idle 600, the red zone from 1900 of 2500 and 80 km/h. The row says what Auto is for the driven truck, such as Auto (Scout). Pick Truck, Scout or Heavy to overrule it. A picked class is saved as `Class`, 0 to 2, and Auto has no key. The class picked while no truck is driven acts only for a truck whose type and weight are not read, and with no truck driven.
- **A class saved by an older version is not read: pick it again if you picked it on purpose.** Older versions wrote the class with every set of scales (`TachClass`), picked or not, so the mod cannot tell a choice from a default. Those keys are now read as Auto, in `Gauges.ini`, in the truck files and in profiles. The numbers saved beside them stay. The case to know: a truck that is no scout by its type, saved with `TachClass=1` and `IdleRpm=0` or `RedlineRpm=0`, had the red zone from 5500 rpm and now has it from 2200 until you pick Scout again. The log has one line for each file that holds the old key. The old key goes out of a file the next time its scales are saved. No file is written at the first start.
- **The scales are kept by truck, not by kind.** A change of the class or of a scale number on the Engine tab is now saved for that one truck, in its section `[Truck:<id>]` of the file of its kind, as the diff types are. Before, it went to every truck of the kind. Scales saved for a kind by an older version stay in force for every truck of the kind without its own. The first change on a truck gives it all seven as its own. The Scales section says whose scales are in force. Its button reads "Scales of the kind" where the truck has its own and "Plain scales" where the kind has them. Import for this truck in the Profiles section now takes the class and scales too.
- **Winch out: a key that lets the winch wire out.** The Controls tab has a row Winch out and under it Winch out speed (`WinchOutRate`, 0.1 to 2.0 m/s, 0.5 by default). The row has no key until you bind one (`BindWinchOut`). Hold it with the winch hooked and the wire is let out, up to the full length of the winch. The wire looks tight while it is let out. It goes slack once the truck moves off. The next pull of the winch takes the slack back, and the game may take it back sooner. It does nothing while the winch pulls and nothing on a crane. Tried in single player alone. On a game version where the mod does not find what it needs, the row is greyed and says so. With no key bound nothing of it runs.
- **The diff types are kept by truck, not by kind.** A change of the Diff row or of an Axle row on the Power tab is now saved for that one truck, in a section `[Truck:<id>]` of the file of its kind. Before, every truck of a kind shared them. A truck never set goes by its kind as before, so nothing changes at the update: what was set before stays for every truck of the kind until you change it on one of them. The diff types follow the truck through a recovery and a garage visit, as its suspension does. Every setting back to its default takes them out for the truck and for its kind. `Gauges.log` says at each truck change where the diff types come from. In a truck profile, Import now takes the diff types for this truck too. A backup of the kind takes `DiffType`, the drag area and the air axles out again where the kind had none. For every truck of this kind says so where this truck keeps settings of its own in force.
- **Truck profiles: the settings of a truck as one file.** The Engine tab has a Profiles section at its foot. Type a name and press Export. The mod writes the settings in force for the driven truck into `Gauges\profiles\<truck> - <name>.ini`: the class and scales, the diff types, the drag area, the suspension, Air brakes and the engine settings. No odometer count and nothing the mod learned goes in. The list under it shows the files of the folder. Import takes what the truck keeps as its own: the diff types, the suspension, Air brakes and the engine settings. The settings of the kind are left. For every truck of this kind takes all of it for the kind. A backup is written before each Import and it is in the list, so taking the backup is the way back. A file from a friend goes into the same folder. A profile made with another engine leaves the engine settings and one made with another gearbox leaves the gear hold. The default of an engine is written only with the box Also the default of the engine ticked. A profile of another truck is under Other trucks and needs a second press. The tire mode is written into the file and is not taken yet.
- Checkers on the wheel, as promised.
- Positions by gearbox follows the shifter of the game on every truck. On some trucks the mod read the flags of the fitted gearbox from another place than the shifter of the game does. The mod then had L-, L+ or H where the shifter shows none. Now the mod takes the five flags as the game hands them to its own shifter at each truck change. Where none comes within two seconds the read of before is taken. Where the box is ticked with a truck already driven, that truck keeps the read of before until the next truck change. `Gauges.log` names the read in force, and both sets of flags where the two differ. A gearbox whose low range the game drives by hand keeps L- and L+, as before.
- The winch line under Physics rate reads "not corrected, see the log" wherever the pull is not corrected at 120. Where the code of the game was left part changed it says to start the game again.
- After a change of truck the over-rev damage and the slip room choice of a held gear could go by the last figures of the truck before, for one pass of the mod. They now wait for the first figures of the truck driven.
- Probe lines no longer print figures of the truck before or figures not read yet: the wheel count of the diff lock bite, the governor part of the torque cut line, the suspension line. The differential line says whether the axle rows make it go by axle. The winch out line says not read where no call was made.
- The winch pulls as hard at `PhysicsRate=120` as at 60. The game limits the pull of the winch by how far the rope closes in one physics step. At 120 steps a second that let the winch haul a truck twice as fast with the same force, and the pull built up much sooner. The mod now gives the game half the distance a step at 120, so the pull is cut at the same speed again and builds up as at 60. The rope and the reel are not changed. Where the code of the game is not found, 120 runs without this and the Physics rate row says so. The test mode is still a test.
- The needle moves while the wheels spin past the top of a held gear. In L-, L, L+, R and a gear with slip room the wheels may run well past the top of the gear. The needle stood at its stop for all of that. Now the top of the gear is the start of the red zone and the fastest the wheels may turn is the stop, and the needle shows where the wheels are between the two. Lift the pedal and it falls with the wheels. The tachometer and the engine sound of the game follow. The travel is small, since it all lies inside the red zone. Under the top of the gear nothing changes, and over-rev damage counts as before. With `Probe=1` the line of the engine speed says which rule gave the needle.
- **A truck with no scales of its own has air brakes by what it is, not by the Truck class row.** Until the Air brakes box is set for a truck, every truck but a scout has them. What counts as a scout is now the class the mod automatic goes by for that truck: the class of its own scales, else its type in the game files, else its weight. Before, the plain Truck class row decided for every truck without scales. So a scout no longer shows the brake air dial and has no spring brakes while the row says Truck, and a truck keeps them while the row says Scout. The box overrules it either way.
- The dials stay drawn while the menu shows the tab of the tuning tool that is not part of a release. Under every other tab of another mod they hide as before.
- The quick winch of the game only while the truck stands. The game puts the quick winch on a key that has another function too. A new row on the Controls tab, Quick winch, sets it: As the game, Standing only or Off (`QuickWinch` 0, 1 or 2). **Standing only is the default from this update:** on the move the key does its other function alone, and the quick winch attaches only under 0.5 m/s. Off never gives a quick winch from that key. The winch menu is not touched. Only the truck you drive is held to the setting. On a game version where the mod does not find the quick winch the row is greyed and the game does as it does.
- The two side buttons of the mouse can be bound on the Controls tab like a key: for shifting up and down, the clutch, a gearbox position or any other action. They show as Mouse 4 and Mouse 5 (`Key Mouse 4`, `Key Mouse 5` in the ini). The left, right and middle buttons still cannot be bound. The game keeps reading the side buttons too: where the game has the same button bound, both act outside the menu.
- A scout is told by the game's own truck type, not by its weight. Every truck file names its type. A truck whose file says `SCOUT` is a scout for the mod automatic whatever it weighs. A truck with another type is never a scout: it is a truck or a heavy truck by its weight, as before. Where the type cannot be read the weight decides alone, as before. **A few trucks change class with this update: heavy scouts become scouts, and some light trucks are no longer taken for scouts.** For them the hold in each gear and the engine's torque curve change with the class. A truck with scales of its own keeps the class of its Truck class row. The Engine and Gearbox tabs say when the class comes from the type. The log has one line a truck: the type read, the class by the type, the class by the weight.
- A locked differential limits the steering, as on a real truck. While an axle that steers is locked the wheels turn to 60 percent of their full angle. While another driven axle is locked they turn to 85 percent. With both locked the smaller figure counts. Two new rows on the Power tab set the two figures: Steering with front lock and Steering with rear lock (`LockSteerFront`, `LockSteerRear`, 30 to 100). At 100 a row leaves all of the steering. With both at 100 nothing is changed. Locked means the game's diff lock is on, or an axle's row or key holds it Locked in a gear position of the mod's gearbox. **A truck whose diff lock is always on counts as locked all the time. From this update it steers to 60 percent with the default figures, or to 85 percent where its front axle is not driven.** Set the two rows to taste, or to 100 for the steering of before. The limit comes and goes over half a second. It never turns the wheels while the stick is at the middle. The steering wheel in the cab still turns fully. The Drive display shows the steering left after the axle types. Needs Real engine. Single player.
- Air brakes by truck. The Engine tab has a box Air brakes for the driven truck, saved for that truck (`AirBrakes` in its file). Until it is set, every truck but a scout has them, as before. Off: the truck has no brake air. No air dial, no LOW BRAKE AIR, no air dryer sound and no spring brakes. On: all of these, and spring brakes. When the brake air falls under 3 bar the truck is held as with the handbrake, and it is let go when the air is back at 4.5 bar. The handbrake key cannot free it before that. A handbrake you set yourself stays on after the release. With the Layout at Off a red box SPRING BRAKES ON takes the place of LOW BRAKE AIR while the truck is held. The tanks of a truck start full, so nothing is held at a start. The hold comes with the engine off and the brake pressed about fifteen times, or by fanning the brake faster than the compressor fills. Not tried in co-op.
- The differential by axle. Under Diff the Power tab has a row for each axle of the driven truck that can be driven, front to back: Axle 1, Axle 2 and on. Each row has the four choices of Diff, so the rear axles can be Locked while the front stays Open. The rows are kept in the truck's file (`DiffAxle1` to `DiffAxle8`). The Diff row sets every axle at once and reads By axle where they differ. A file from before reads as every axle on its `DiffType`, so nothing changes at the update. A Locked axle beside another type is tied only with Diff lock bite above 0. While the game's diff lock is on every axle is locked. Two new bindings under Locks and ride, Lock front axle and Lock rear axles, hold those axles Locked until pressed again. They are unbound by default and are not saved. The Drive display names each axle's type where they differ. A truck whose wheels the mod cannot pair gets no rows and goes by the Diff row as before.
- The Display tab has a fifth Layout: Off (`Layout=4`). No dials are drawn. Every feature acts as before and the NEXT GEN menu stays. The rows that only shape the dials are greyed, and the toggle key does nothing until another layout is picked. An older version of the mod reads 4 as the strip.
- With the Layout at Off a row Warnings chooses what still warns (`HiddenWarnings`). None: no box, no lamp and no warning sound, while the damage still acts. As with dials, the default: the clutch and engine boxes. At my limits: a box when a figure passes a limit set on a slider. The limits are fuel under (`WarnFuelUnder`, 15 percent), engine damage over and gearbox damage over (`WarnEngineOver`, `WarnGearboxOver`, 50 percent) and coolant over (`WarnCoolantOver`, 90 percent of the way from 85 C to the red mark). Each slider has an off end. Oil warns at `OilWarnC`, tilt at its two angles and brake air under 5.5 bar. New boxes: LOW FUEL, ENGINE WORN, GEARBOX WORN, OIL HOT, TILT and LOW BRAKE AIR. At most three boxes show at once, the red ones first. With dials shown nothing changes.
- The dials and the Gameplay tab could be missing for a whole session: on some systems the game is ready sooner than the mod was. The mod now sets up its drawing as the first thing at its start. If the game was sooner still, another Next Gen mod of this release in the game hands the picture over to it. With no such mod the log says after ten seconds, in capitals, that the overlay draws nothing and why, and the mod draws as soon as the game sets up its picture anew.
- If another mod changes the physics rate of the game, a red sign in the middle of the screen says so until the game ends: "Another mod changes the physics rate. Turn that mod off and start the game again." No key and no setting hides it. This mod's figures are for 60 steps a second, and at another rate its engine pulls wrong. Nothing else changes, and Gauges.log has one line with the step it read. The check needs the hook on the wheels' drive, which is in with `Powertrain`, `Creep`, `Probe`, a suspension feature or the drive display on. With all of them off the log says once that the step is not checked.
- The mod publishes four figures of the Powertrain in a block of shared memory (`src\ng_tune.h`), for a tuning tool that is not part of a release: Diff lock bite, Low range slip, and the ini keys `WheelCap` and `PushShare`. A figure the tool asks for goes in as an edit on the tab does. Without the tool nothing reads the block.
- L, L- and R pull as hard as L+ from a standstill (`LowRangeSlip`, the Low range slip row on the Power tab, 0 to 100, 100 by default). In this game a wheel pulls by its slip against the ground. The wheel cap held each wheel to a share of its gear's top speed, so the lower the gear the less slip and the less pull: on soft ground L- gave about a third of what L+ gave. Now the cap of L and L- has the room of L+ while the truck is slower than a quarter of the gear's top speed. From there the extra room shrinks and at three quarters of the top it is gone, so each gear's top speed and the steady needle there are as before. The cost: standing or crawling in L-, a wheel in the air or on ice may turn as fast as a wheel in L+ may, which is 5 to 7 times the top of L-. That is not the runaway spin, and the cap still ends it at the same speed as in L+. At 0 the cap is as before. R and L+ are unchanged. R has the same room: before, the wheels hardly spun in reverse. The cost is the same in R: a wheel in the air may turn as fast as one in L+ while the truck is slow.
- Drive display: "diff" now marks only a wheel the differential setting moved by more than 0.2 rad/s and more than 5 percent of its speed. Before, with the differential Open, it was lit on every wheel all the time.
- Probe (`Probe=1`): the diff lock line also counts the wheels written under 0 and under what the drive left them while the drive pushes forward. It reads 0 while the lock's bounds hold. The by-wheel line adds each wheel's speed after the wheel cap, the cap's ceiling of that step and three ground figures, and its truck speed no longer reads "nan" when the lock runs without the cut.
- At `Gearbox=2` a press of the shifter button no longer shifts the gearbox. The game sends the position its shifter stands at again each time the shifter button is let go, and the mod read every such send as a pick. So with the shifter at A, holding the button for the diff lock or for a binding with that button in it put the gearbox in A, out of the low range or a held gear. Now the mod keeps the last position the shifter sent and follows only one that differs. A binding does not change the kept one. The cost: the position the shifter already stands at cannot be picked on it again. Its binding picks it, or move the shifter to another position first. `IgnoreShifter=1` still turns the shifter off altogether. `Gauges.log` says "the game's shifter sent A again, not followed" the first time for each position and gives a count a minute after. Each position line in the log now names what moved the gearbox: the bindings, the game's shifter, the sequential, the gearbox damage or the gearbox's positions.
- Drive display: the mark at the truck speed goes by the main body's forward direction. Before, it went by the part the wheel hangs on, which can swing on its own. At a truck change two trucks can no longer fill the display together: the call that comes second runs with no display.
- Drive display, a new box beside the suspension display (`DriveDisplay`, the Drive display row on the Power tab, off by default). It shows the truck from above with a cell for each wheel, up to 16. The left bar is the wheel speed at the ground against the truck speed, with a mark at the truck speed: amber past 15 percent, red at the end of the bar, blue when the wheel turns against the gear. The right bar is the share of the drive on that wheel in percent, with a thin mark for the share before the wheel cap and the differential acted. "air" marks a wheel off the ground, "cap" a wheel the cap holds and "diff" a wheel the differential setting moved. The line under the wheels names the differential in force and what the cap kept. It only reads and changes nothing in the drive. The setting is for the player, not for a truck.
- Diff lock bite no longer turns a wheel backward or spins one up while the engine pulls. Before, with the truck held against an obstacle in a forward gear, the lock could write a wheel under 0 or faster than any wheel the game drive had left. Now three bounds hold on what the lock writes while the drive pushes forward: no wheel under 0 unless the drive left it there, no wheel faster than the fastest the drive left, and no spin added in sum. What the bounds do not let through is dropped. A truck rolling back with the engine pulling is as before. With `Probe=1` the line a second also counts the steps where a bound acted, and a second line gives the last written step wheel by wheel.
- Spring and Damping are set apart for the front and the rear: Spring front, Spring rear, Damping front and Damping rear (`SpringFront`, `SpringRear`, `DampingFront`, `DampingRear`). Front is the first axle, rear every axle behind it, a second steered axle too. A trailer is not touched. Settings from before carry over: `SpringScale` and `DampingScale` read as both ends, in `Gauges.ini` and in every truck's file, so nothing changes at the update. A save writes the new keys. It keeps the old key while both ends are alike and removes it once they differ. `Gauges.log` names each truck's front and rear wheels once.
- The physics rate's test mode (`PhysicsRate=120`) takes a world's first step as good within 20 percent of 1/120 s. Before, it had to be 1/120 s to the last bit, and the world's own figure is a hair off, so the line in capitals and the tab's note came on a world that did run at 120. The iterations are still checked exactly, and the log line gives the step's bits.
- A key of the top row bound to an action is the same key after a restart. Before, the 1 key was written `Key 1` and read back as the left mouse button, so a click selected 1st gear. `Key 0` was refused. An ini written so heals by itself. A mouse button in the ini is refused with one line in `Gauges.log`, as the tab never binds one.
- Gear 1's second binding has a key of its own, `BindGear1Second`. Before, it shared `BindGear12` with Gear 12's first, and after a restart both held Gear 12's control. Set Gear 1's second binding once more on the tab where it had one.
- A change of one binding on the tab writes that one line of `Gauges.ini`.
- The automatic heals from a wheel speed that is no number. Before, one such reading as a truck was taken kept the box in 1st until another truck.
- A truck just switched to no longer gets the last truck's suspension for a moment. Before, the damping, the spring, the frame flex and the camber of the last truck went into the new one for two passes, and for up to twenty on a truck known by its build only. Now they wait until the new truck's own settings are in force.
- The cluster hides while the NEXT GEN menu shows another mod's tab, so it no longer lies over that tab. On the Gameplay tabs and the Graphics tab it stays, so a change to Size or the layout shows at once.

- A long tip on the Gameplay tab is wrapped and stays inside the screen. Before, a line ran past the screen edge and was cut.
- A settings save no longer stops the mod for up to a second. Gauges.ini is written by a thread of its own, one save at a time with the newest values, so the needles, the torque cut and the engine speed go on meanwhile. Reload ini waits for a running save. The bindings go the same way.
- The texts of the menu tabs and the cluster carry no apostrophe and no contraction: "Game gearbox", "does not". A test keeps them so.
- The Graphics tab's text no longer says how Next Gen Graphics' keys behave beside it; the DLL's own lines in the tab say it.
- The crash line in `Gauges.log` is armed as the mod starts, so a fault, a fast fail or an exit at the game's boot is named too. Before, it was armed only at the first truck. It is armed again there, after the game has set its own handlers, so they do not push it out. At the first truck the log counts the faults and C++ throws of the boot, and the first three of each are named again from there. A handler set in between that passes on to the mod's never calls it in a loop: the second call goes to the handler from the start, or ends there.
- A truck taken out of the warehouse gets its own settings back. The game gives it a new id there. Where exactly one waiting truck of its kind has the same engine and gearbox, its suspension and engine sets are copied to the new id. Waiting means not driven this start, or put away since with no loading screen between. The old sections stay, marked `How=warehouse`, and are never dropped. A truck bought new with the same engine and gearbox as one waiting truck gets a copy of that set too, and the other keeps its own. Two such trucks or none leave it on its kind's settings. `[Seen]` in each kind's file lists every truck driven by its id, so a truck that never had a set of its own never takes another's. A truck's own sections now hold `Engine=` and `Gearbox=`. Files from before read as they did, and nothing matches until a truck has been driven once with this version. `Gauges.log` has one line a drive: the truck's own settings and when it was last driven, the kind's and why, taken over, or matched out of the warehouse. The Suspension and Engine tabs say which.
- The take-over, the marks and the match are in `truck_id.h`, the same file as Next Gen Tire Inflation's, so both mods name and follow a truck alike.
- Spring and Damping are written in every physics step, from the hook on the game's wheels' drive, so a wheel's spring and damping change in the same step. A value the game sets itself, an upgrade or a repair, is kept as the truck's own and the sliders go on from it. Before, it could be written over with the old figure. `Gauges.log` names the threads the drive runs on, once.
- An odometer under the speed in every layout: the kilometres each truck has driven, miles with Mph. Each truck keeps its own count by its id, and the count follows it through a recovery, the garage and the warehouse as its settings do. A kind of truck that works standing can count engine hours instead: Counts in the Display tab. Set to writes a figure for this truck, for one bought used, and 0 starts it anew. Odometer in the Display tab turns the readout off. Only real driving counts: nothing while the game is held, on the map or in the garage. The count is saved every minute, at a stop and when a menu opens.
- A truck out of the warehouse with no settings of its own takes its count from the one waiting truck of its kind with the same engine and gearbox. A truck with settings is matched first, as before.
- A truck back from the garage keeps its settings and its count when it is the truck that went last, with no other truck driven and no loading screen between, however long the visit. The garage gives it a new id on a new vehicle. Before, a second waiting truck with the same engine and gearbox stopped the match. A section made after the first stop, such as the engine's after the odometer's, now gets the engine and gearbox at the next look, so the match can go by it. `Gauges.log` writes one `match:` line at each match, with what each truck of the kind has and why it was taken or not. The `truck:` line says what was taken: the truck that went last, a match by its parts, or a count without settings. With `Probe=1` the game's state is written at each change while no truck is read, the garage's pointer with it.
- A gearbox name with spaces is read as the name, as a mod's gearbox may have. Before, it read as no name, and the gearbox went by its gears. A gearbox whose gears Next Gen Tire Inflation moves stays one gearbox: their tops stand in one ratio, the tire's roll. Before, the moved gears could make a second gearbox with its own file and its hold back at the class's, and a match out of the warehouse or the garage could miss when it read the gears before the tire moved them. A truck recorded by its gears matches its gearbox's name read later where those gears are the truck's, so the records turn to the name with no lost match.
- In a gear held by hand the truck pulls with the mod engine alone (Real engine and Mod engine speed on, the mod gearbox, Engine share at 100). The game gives its torque by how far the wheels are toward the gear's top: all of it at about half and nothing at the top. It cut its pull there and gave it back only well under it, so floored in L- the truck climbed to the top, sagged and climbed again every few seconds, with the game's own gearbox too. Now the mod answers the game's torque call for its wheels: the mod engine's curve at the engine speed the needle shows, and none of the game's fall. Floored in L- the needle climbs to the redline and holds. In deep mud a wheel that breaks loose no longer runs away: the game's own brake on a wheel past the gear's top acts again, and after a cut the pull comes back at the pace of the engine's response, as in the game. So in deep mud the low range pulls a little softer. In a gear held by hand the governor goes by the speed of the truck itself, not by the wheels, so the engine holds the truck at the top speed of the gear as a governor does, and a wheel that spins or hops no longer makes the pull jump. The speed of the truck turns into a wheel speed by the radius the wheels roll on, each wheel's collision cylinder, which the tire mod sets with the pressure. So at a low pressure the gear tops out where it really does, and the over-rev damage starts where it should. A wheel without a cylinder, or with one under half or over 1.25 of the wheel's own radius, goes by its own radius, and `Gauges.log` says how many did. ## [0.12.3]

- The mod's automatic goes by the pedal where your foot holds it. It went by the pedal after the powertrain's curve, under which a fifth of the pedal reads as a twentieth, so a light foot sat on the line between coasting and driving and it shifted 4 to 5 and back every few seconds.
- A tap of the shift modifier with the foot off no longer drops the truck into first at speed. The game's own automatic picks first then whatever the speed (at 23 km/h, about 8400 rpm), and the mod took that gear on; it now takes the lowest gear the engine can take at the speed the wheels turn.

## [0.12.2]

- The mod's automatic no longer shifts down into a gear that races the engine. In 0.12.1 it learned how fast the wheels turn for the truck's speed from any moment with the foot on, so a bump or the truck sliding back down a slope could teach it the truck went at half its speed: it then shifted down as far as first at up to 6800 rpm, and held first on the limiter for half a minute. It learns now only while the wheels turn steadily and alike, the truck going forwards with no brake or handbrake, and follows a lower reading over two seconds. It goes by the wheels again and weighs them against the ground only to tell wheelspin and locked wheels, and no shift down takes the engine past nine tenths of the way to the red zone at the speed the wheels turn.
- A climb is told by the truck losing speed for half a second with the foot on, where a moment of it was enough, so the shift points no longer jump to floored's on a bump.
- A tap of the shift modifier the game goes back on within two seconds (it picked 3rd from 1st and fell back to 1st) is taken on as well; before, it gave the gears back as if picked by hand.

## [0.12.1]

- The mod's automatic shifts sooner. Floored it shifts up at about four fifths of the way from idle to the red zone, where it waited until nearly the red zone (on the Defender 4040 rpm, where it was about 4600 from 4th to 5th), and at a light foot lower still. The shift points are measured from idle, so a slow revving diesel and a fast revving petrol engine both shift in their band.
- With the foot off it no longer shifts up (only past the red zone, rolling downhill), and it shifts down early, at a quarter of the way from idle to the red zone, gears at a time when the truck slows hard, never so far that the engine races. Before, a lift shifted up and the shifts down came late, the last one as the truck stopped.
- Climbing (the truck slowing with the foot on) it shifts down where floored would and holds those points four seconds after, so it does not shift up and straight back down. Before, it barely shifted on a climb: wheels slipping held every shift.
- It goes by the truck's speed over the ground, learned against the wheels while they grip, so wheelspin in mud does not shift it up and wheels locked under the brake do not throw it into first; the shifts no longer wait while the wheels slip.
- A push of the pedal to the floor kicks down at once, once; held floored it steps down a gear at a time as the truck slows.
- Tapping the shift modifier while the mod drives lets the game's own automatic pick a gear, which the mod takes on and holds for two seconds. Before, the mod set its own gear straight back, and a tap for 5th went back to 4th.
- Another mod raising the power factor in A (a manual transmission mod sets 1.05) no longer reads as a low gear picked by hand, which gave the gears back a few seconds after they were taken.

## [0.12.0]

- The mod's automatic drives the game's gears. `Gearbox=1` (the tab's new Gearbox section) hands the gears to the mod's automatic while the game's shifter is in A: a torque converter (the engine at its stall speed with the pedal floored against a standing truck, at most 2400 rpm, following the road speed with a little slip on the move, locked up from the second gear), shift points that rise with the pedal, a kickdown when floored, the gear held when the foot lifts and rolling downhill, no shift in wheelspin, and the first gear by the time the truck stops. The tachometer and everything that goes by it (the turbo, the heat) take the converter's engine speed, and the cluster shows A. The gears are set through the game's own shift function with the game's automatic put down; picking a gear by hand gives the gearbox back, A takes it again. The truck still pulls with the game's own torque. `Gearbox=0`, the default, leaves the game's automatic as it was.
- The converter's stall speed is at most 2400 rpm: a fast revving engine stops well short of its red zone floored against a standing truck.

- The placeholder sections of 0.11.0 to 0.11.2 are renamed to the game's names with the temperatures they hold, and the four regions they lacked (Quebec, Washington, Chiapas, Zurdania) are added.

Nothing of it reaches the game yet.

## [0.11.1]

- The heat comes from the fuel the engine burns: the pedal and the engine speed together, as in a real engine. Floored at the red zone makes the most heat, floored at low revs little, and coasting or rolling downhill with the foot off next to none. In 0.11.0 a truck coasting at high revs heated as if floored.
- The cold engine damage goes by the fuel burned too: only a cold engine burning more than half of what it burns floored at the red zone suffers. Pulling away and driving about at moderate revs cost nothing, also on a keyboard.
- The oil takes its own heat: the friction, which grows with the engine speed, and the pistons' cooling, which grows with the load. It runs about 7 degrees over the coolant at cruise and 14 floored at the red zone, and it is warm a minute after the coolant at the least, whatever `OilWarmUpMinutes` says.

- `models_test.exe <drive> <idle> <red zone>` replays a recorded drive on a truck's own scales.

## [0.11.0]

- The coolant temperature follows the engine speed, not the pedal. An engine revved high heats whatever the foot does, one pulling at low revs heats little, and in neutral it makes a quarter of that heat.

- The oil temperature. The oil follows the coolant slowly: driven as usual it is at 85 C after about eight minutes, and a stopped engine's oil keeps its heat longer. It runs about 2 degrees over the coolant at idle and 14 at full load. Each kind of truck keeps its own.

- `BoostProfile`: how the turbo builds its boost. Standard, small and quick (early, little lag), big and slow (late, long to come at low revs), or two stages (a small turbo early and a big one after it). It is on the tab under Scales, and each kind of truck can have its own, as its seventh scale.

## [0.6.0]

- The engine damage lamp blinks while the mod adds damage.
- `Damage` is off by default, and the temperature is then only shown, as before. The Gauges tab has a new Engine damage section with the switch and the two rates, `HotDamagePercentPerMinute` and `ColdDamagePercentPerMinute`.
- The radiator gets all its air from 30 km/h, where it took 50 km/h before. - With `Probe=1` the log has a sixth line with the engine's damage, the engine's record and what the mod adds.
