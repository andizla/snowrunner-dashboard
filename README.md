# Next Gen Gameplay

A gauge cluster for the truck you drive in SnowRunner: analogue dials in the style of a truck dashboard, drawn over the game in the game's own font.

The cluster has a speedometer, a tachometer with the gear, fuel with the range and the fuel used a minute, coolant and oil temperature, boost pressure, brake air pressure, a clock with the game's time of day and the outside temperature, and nine lamps: handbrake, headlights, all wheel drive, differential lock, low gear, engine damage, cold engine, tilt and oil temperature.

This is version 0.18.4. It reads the truck's own values for the speedometer, the gear, the fuel gauge and seven of the lamps: handbrake, headlights, all wheel drive, differential lock, low gear, engine damage and tilt. The game has no engine speed, no range, no coolant or oil temperature, no boost pressure and no brake air, so the mod works those out from what the truck does, and lights the cold and oil lamps by the temperatures. The temperature also costs the engine: overheating it, or working it hard while cold, adds to the game's own engine damage. `Damage=0` switches that off. The scales of the tachometer and the speedometer and the turbo's boost and character can be set for each kind of truck. The brake air comes from the engine's compressor, which fills the tanks up to 8.2 bar and starts again at 6.9, and each press of the brake and letting the handbrake go draw from them; scouts brake by hydraulics, so the air dial is hidden on scout scales (`TachClass=1`, or a truck file that sets it). The outside temperature goes by the map you are on, which the mod reads from the game's save. With `Gearbox=1` an automatic of the mod's own, with a torque converter, picks the gears while the game's shifter is in A, and with `Powertrain=1` the truck pulls with the mod's engine and converter in place of the game's own.

## What you need

- SnowRunner for Windows: the Steam, Epic Games Store or Microsoft Store (Xbox app) version. The mod is made on the Steam build of 22 July 2026 and knows where that build keeps what it reads. On any other build (another store's, or after a game update) it finds those places by the game's code that uses them, and says in its log what it found; where it cannot find the truck it looks again every 2 seconds for a minute (another mod can load it before the game's code is ready), then stands down and changes nothing. Where those miss, it finds the truck's control, the game's state and the wheels' class the ways Next Gen Tire Inflation finds them on the Microsoft Store and Game Pass build, and the log names the way. The signatures it finds them by were taken from the Steam build; another build stands down only where the truck, its wheels or the game's state cannot be found, and its clock is looked for with `Probe=1` (see the outside temperature).
- An ASI loader, which loads `Gauges.asi` when the game starts: the `version.dll` in the release, the same file as Next Gen Tire Inflation's (the SnowRunner Tire Inflation System).
- ReShade 6.8.0, which comes in the zip as `dxgi.dll` and `ReShade.ini`, the same files as Next Gen Tire Inflation's, for compatibility with overlays such as RivaTuner, Discord, Nvidia and Steam. It shows nothing of its own; Home opens its window. A ReShade already in the game works as it is (any ReShade from 6.8.0 on with add-on support). Without ReShade the game can crash with the RivaTuner or Discord overlay or with multiple monitors, and the mod says so at the start.

- The full manual, with every dial, setting and model, as a PDF: [Next Gen Gameplay Manual](https://github.com/andizla/snowrunner-dashboard/blob/main/docs/Next_Gen_Gameplay_Manual.pdf), also [in Russian](https://github.com/andizla/snowrunner-dashboard/blob/main/docs/Next_Gen_Gameplay_Manual_ru.pdf).

## Install

1. Close the game.
2. Copy `Gauges.asi`, `version.dll`, `dxgi.dll` and `ReShade.ini` into the game's `Sources\Bin` folder, next to `SnowRunner.exe`. A `version.dll` already there from Next Gen Tire Inflation is the same loader: keep it; its `dxgi.dll` and `ReShade.ini` are the same files as this release's.
3. Start the game. F2 opens the NEXT GEN menu, and its Gameplay tab has the mod's settings.

On Linux (Proton, the Steam Deck) set SnowRunner's launch options in Steam (right click, Properties, General) to `WINEDLLOVERRIDES="version=n,b" %command%`. Then the game takes this `version.dll` instead of Wine's own. The mod there is not yet tested by a player.

Already using ReShade? Choose Skip for `dxgi.dll` and `ReShade.ini` when Windows asks, and yours stays. The mods draw without ReShade too. It is in the zip for RivaTuner users, whose game does not boot without it. With Spotter installed, the overlay draws through Spotter's frame hook; `NextGen.ini` `[Overlay] Spotter=0` turns it off.

To update, copy the new file over the old one. A `Gauges.addon64` from a test build was loaded by ReShade: delete it when you install `Gauges.asi`. With two files of the mod in the folder only one runs, and the log says which. `Gauges.ini` keeps working.

## What it shows

- Speed, forwards and backwards.
- The gear: its number with an A in automatic, H for the high gear, L, L+ and L- for the low range, R and N.
- The engine speed. The game has none, so the mod works it out from the driven wheels, the gear and the pedal. The needle climbs through each gear to the red zone and falls at the shift. It rises under the pedal when the truck is stuck and revs freely in neutral, and spinning wheels take it into the red zone. It rises from 0 while the engine starts and runs down when the engine stalls or stops.
- The odometer under the speed: the kilometres the truck has driven (miles with Mph), or its engine hours for a kind set to count hours. Each truck keeps its own count in `[Odometer:<id>]` of its kind's file, as `Metres=` and `Seconds=`. Only driving counts, never a held game, the map or the garage, and hours are real running time. It is saved every minute, at a stop and when a menu opens. The count follows the truck through a recovery, the garage and the warehouse as its settings do. A truck bought new with the same engine and gearbox as one waiting truck of its kind takes that truck's count too, as it takes its set: Set to in the Display tab puts it right.
- Fuel as a share of the tank.
- The range: how far the fuel in the tank goes at the litres per kilometre the truck burned on the last three kilometres it drove. It shows `---` until the truck has driven half a kilometre. Fuel burned while standing comes off the range and leaves the litres per kilometre alone. The numbers are small, as SnowRunner's trucks are thirsty: a scout that burns 10 litres a kilometre off road gets about 7 km out of its 75 litre tank.
- Lamps for the handbrake, the headlights, all wheel drive, the differential lock and the low range. The engine damage lamp lights when the game's engine damage is above half of what the engine takes, and it blinks while the mod adds damage and for two seconds after each write.
- With `Damage` on, a warning box at the top left, in the shape of Next Gen Tire Inflation's tire warning and stacked below it (below the clutch's box when that shows too): amber "ENGINE HOT" from the coolant's red zone mark, with the degrees over it as a number and a bar, and red "ENGINE DAMAGE" while the heat costs the engine. And one line from a film when you push it. It is not drawn over the NEXT GEN menu.
- The tilt lamp: a small truck seen from behind that leans as yours does. It is green while the truck stands level, amber from 15 degrees of lean to a side and red from 25, or from 35 degrees up or down a slope. A bump does not light it.
- The coolant temperature. The game has none, so the mod works it out from the fuel the engine burns and how fast the truck drives. A real engine's coolant takes about a quarter to a third of the fuel's heat, and the fuel goes with the pedal and the engine speed together: floored at the red zone makes the most heat, floored at low revs little, and coasting with the foot off next to none, as a diesel burns next to nothing then. In neutral nothing pulls against the engine, and it makes a quarter of the heat. A cold engine starts at the outside temperature and is at 85 C after about three minutes of driving. The thermostat then holds it: about 86 C at idle, near 100 C when the engine works hard at speed. The needle goes into the red zone from 118 C only when the engine works hard at a crawl. Floored at a standstill with the wheels spinning and the engine at the red zone that takes a minute and a half from 90 C, and longer at lower revs, with the pedal not quite floored, or with the truck still moving. From about 7 km/h up the needle stays out of the red zone: a truck in the low range at its own top speed runs hot and settles just under it. Back at idle the engine is at 100 C again within a minute. Water cools it too: from about half a metre of water at the truck's front, and with the engine standing in it (1.2 m and more) a hot engine is back at 100 C within half a minute, at any engine speed. A stopped engine cools from 90 C to 40 C in a quarter of an hour, and five times as fast in deep water. Each kind of truck keeps its own temperature while the game runs: one you left cools while you drive another, and is as warm as that when you take it again, out of the garage too. The mod knows a truck by its kind, so two trucks of the same kind share one temperature. After a loading screen the truck that drives on under the game's id for it keeps its heat: a travel between regions, a border crossing or a reload. A game start, the garage, a recovery or a change of truck makes every engine as cold as the air outside.
- The cold lamp is lit while the coolant is below 60 C.
- The oil temperature, as a second, amber needle in the coolant's dial or on a dial of its own left of it. In a real engine the coolant takes its heat from the cylinders, and the oil takes the heat of the friction in the bearings and the pistons and of the jets that cool the pistons, and gives it to the coolant through the oil cooler. So the oil follows the coolant slowly and runs a few degrees over it: about 7 degrees over at cruise and 14 floored at the red zone, more the faster the engine turns and the harder it works. Driven as usual the oil is at 85 C after about five minutes, two after the coolant, and never before the coolant. A stopped engine's oil keeps its heat longer. Each kind of truck keeps its own, as with the coolant.
- The oil lamp blinks while the oil is too hot, from 120 C, and a warning sounds every four seconds until the oil is two degrees under that or the engine stops.
- Boost pressure. The game has none, so the mod runs a turbo of its own. Its rotor spools by the fuel the engine burns and how fast it turns: slowly low down and at a light foot, quickly high up. A truck's diesel takes about 2 s to its boost at 1100 rpm and under a second at 1760. Floored, a truck's diesel makes all its boost from about 1300 rpm and a scout's petrol from about 1650; at part load the boost goes by the fuel. On a short lift the rotor keeps turning, so the boost is back at once; it runs down over a second and a half. In neutral the free engine makes little boost, and on the governor the boost falls with the fuel. How much a truck's turbo reaches is the setting `BoostMax`: trucks 1.8 bar, scouts 1, heavy trucks 2. How it gets there is `BoostProfile`: the class's own, a small quick one that is full sooner with half the lag, a big slow one that comes in later with more, or two stages, full sooner with 1.3 times the boost and a short dip at the changeover. Trucks and heavy trucks are diesels and scouts petrol, unless an engine's profile says otherwise (`Fuel`, below). The dial reads the manifold's pressure. A diesel's starts at 0. A petrol engine's starts at -1 bar (-15 psi), as its throttle holds the manifold under the atmosphere at idle (about -0.7 bar) and at a light foot. The dial ends at 2 bar (30 psi), or at 3 bar (45 psi) for a turbo of more than 2.
- The turbo heard on a lift. A petrol engine's throttle shuts against the boost, and the air beats back through the compressor wheel, slower and fainter until it is gone: a flutter, or a blow-off on a full lift. A diesel has no throttle plate and no blow-off valve, so its turbo just coasts down and its lift is quiet, until proper turbo sounds come. Your own `sounds\turbo_whistle_` files play there (below). `DieselFlutter=1` (the Sounds tab's "Diesel flutter") has a diesel flutter as a petrol engine does. The more boost the lift had, the longer and louder, and no two in a row are the same. It stops when the pedal goes down again.
- Engine damage from the temperature. An engine in the red zone loses 10 percent of what it can take every minute at 125 C. It loses nothing at 118 C, less than that between the two and more above: floored at a standstill with the wheels spinning, an engine is used up about five minutes after it enters the red zone. A cold engine suffers when it works hard: below 60 C, burning more than half the fuel it burns floored at the red zone, it loses up to 5 percent a minute (floored at the red zone at 0 C), and less the warmer it is and the lighter the work. A cold start floored at the red zone costs 2 to 3 percent of the engine; driving about at moderate revs costs nothing, floored or not, as the engine burns little fuel there. `ColdDamagePercentPerMinute` set to 0 switches that part off. A gear held by hand driven past the red zone costs the engine too, as after a downshift at speed or down a long grade: with the torque cut acting and the clutch in, once the truck runs past the governor's end for half a second, up to 10 percent a minute at 0.2 of the gear's top past that end (`OverRevDamagePercentPerMinute`, Over-rev damage on the Engine tab, 0 is off). It goes by the truck's own speed held to the wheels' speed, so wheels spinning in mud cost nothing, and never in A. Above the redline the engine braking keeps rising up to 1.3 of the gear's top, so an over-revved engine holds the truck harder. With Engine braking at 0 the engine does not hold the truck and a descent in a held gear over-revs. All of it goes into the game's own engine damage, so a garage repairs it, and past the engine's own threshold the game takes torque away and burns more fuel, as with any engine damage. The mod stops one point short of what the engine takes and never stops the engine itself. With `Damage` off the temperature is only shown.

The cluster is on screen while you drive a truck in the world, and it fades in and out. It is hidden on loading screens, in the pause menu, on the map and in the garage.

## Settings

The Gameplay tab of the NEXT GEN menu edits every setting while you play, in eight tabs: Display, Engine, Gearbox, Power, Suspension, Controls, Sounds and Mod work. It opens on the tab open last. F2 opens and closes the menu; another key is set by clicking the key's name at the menu's top right and pressing it, or in `NextGen.ini` next to the exe (`[Menu]`, `Key=` a virtual key code), which the Next Gen mods share. While the menu is open the game gets no mouse and no keys, and the cluster's toggle key and pad toggle rest. Changes count at once and are saved to `Gauges.ini` in the same folder. The game writes that file with the defaults and a short guide on its first start, so it can be edited by hand too. With Next Gen Graphics installed the menu also has a Graphics tab. It switches Graphics' effects while you play. Next Gen Graphics' own lines in the tab say which of its keys work beside it.

The plain scales are the ones in the ini's `[Gauges]` section. IdleRpm=0

`Gauges.log` names the truck each time you take another one. A file can be written by hand as well, and a key it leaves out is taken from `[Gauges]`. The Suspension tab works the same way: change a setting there while you drive, and that kind of truck keeps the whole set as its own in a `[Suspension]` section of the same file (the box Own settings ticks itself). The old section is then marked `TakenBy=<new id>` and `TakenAt`, and only the newest 10 marked sections of a file are kept. Every truck of the kind with a set for the kind already puts that set in force and keeps the truck's with `Own=0`. A truck taken out of the warehouse gets a new id. The Engine tab's Engine torque and the Gearbox tab's Upshift delay work the same way, with the Engine tab's own box Own settings and For. Make this the engine's default writes the torque to `Gauges\engines\<name>.ini` and the hold to `Gauges\gearboxes\<name>.ini`, for every truck without its own. A figure the truck has none for comes from those files, then 100 % and the class's hold. The line In force under each slider says which. The engine's profile is kept the same way, as `PeakTorqueRpm.<engine>=`, `Aspiration.<engine>=`, `Fuel.<engine>=` and `TorqueCurve.<engine>=`, but whole: the level that holds any of them gives all four, and a key it lacks is the stock one, not the next level's. Every other setting is the same for all trucks. A `Gauges.ini` from a test build may still hold a truck's scales as a section such as `[Truck:chevrolet_ck1500]`: the first start moves them into their files.

The mod keeps the place in `Gauges.ini` under `[Clock]`; until it is found the day's mean is in force. The tab shows the day's and the night's temperature and the hour of the game's day.

Each of the three coolant times changes one thing: how fast a cold engine warms up, how fast a hot one overheats, how fast a stopped one cools. The temperatures the engine settles at stay as they are. The outside temperature moves those: in frost the engine takes longer to warm up and cannot be overheated, and on a hot day it overheats sooner. `AirFullKmh` moves them at a crawl: lower keeps a slow, hard pulling engine cooler, and higher makes it overheat at speeds where it now stays out of the red zone.

The cluster keeps its share of the screen: it is twice as large in pixels on a 2160p screen as on a 1080p one.

The flutter comes in three strengths, for a lift at full boost, at half and at little, with three recordings of each. To hear recordings of your own instead, put them in the game's `Sources\Bin` folder as `sounds\turbo_high_1.wav` to `turbo_high_4.wav`, and `turbo_mid_` and `turbo_low_` likewise: WAV files, PCM or float, up to six seconds. A strength with at least one file of yours plays only yours. The oil warning is two short beeps, and `sounds\oil_beep_1.wav` plays in their place. Each time the governor stops the compressor at the cut-out, the air dryer purges with a short hiss, made by the mod's code, or `sounds\air_purge_1.wav` to `_4.wav` in its place. A diesel's lift has no sound of the mod's: `sounds\turbo_whistle_1.wav` to `_4.wav` of your own play there, at a pitch set by the turbo's speed at the lift, and without them it is quiet. The mod brings every sound to the same loudness, and `Gauges.log` says which ones it plays. The Gameplay tab has a button for each strength and one for the oil warning.

The pad is only read. The game sees the toggle buttons too, so pick buttons it does not use while driving.

## The mod's automatic

The game's automatic shifts up at fixed speeds whatever the pedal, never shifts down under load until the engine bogs, and then drops to first. `Gearbox=1` (the Gearbox tab's "Mod automatic", where A is called D) hands the gears to an automatic of the mod's own while the game's shifter is in A:

- a torque converter, as a truck's: standing with the pedal floored the engine stops at the converter's stall speed (0.8 of the red zone, at most 2600 rpm), and through a launch it climbs from there as the truck catches up, the converter doubling the torque at the stall and less as it couples (at 0.87). A launch locks once the converter has all but coupled, so the lock drops the needle a little; a shift made locked locks again at once, so from there the gears go lock to lock. Locked, it stays locked on a lift, so the engine follows the wheels and brakes the truck, and a press never opens it: one that needs more pull gets a shift down. It opens near idle, at a stop and for the launch. At idle in gear the converter creeps: the truck moves off with the needle at idle and holds about walking pace (Creep at idle below);
- shifts up later the further the pedal is down. A light foot held steady cruises: it shifts up as soon as the next gear lands at the light-foot shift-up point or over (with the mod's engine, 0.8 of where its curve first reaches nine tenths of its torque, no later than 0.45 of the red zone), so the engine settles low at a steady speed, as a road car's automatic does. Lifting at speed shifts up a gear at a time where the next lands clear of the coast point (driven with the keyboard, once the key has been let go 2 seconds, and a shift up the next press or the brake takes straight back stops that for 10 seconds, then 20, up to a minute, so a key tapped to hold the speed keeps the gear); rolling downhill or slowing hard (mud, a climb) it keeps the gear, for the engine's braking. Coasting it holds the gear down to 0.15 of the way from idle to the red zone (0.25 with the brake on, so the engine brakes), then shifts down, gears at a time when the truck slows hard. Floored it takes the gear that pulls the most in the game: the game gives the engine's torque by how far the wheels are toward the gear's top speed, all of it at about half and nothing at the top, so floored it shifts up at about two thirds to four fifths of the gear's top and down where the gear below pulls more. Each gear's top is worked out from the gearbox's own number for it; a floored plateau, the game's pull at nothing with the wheels steady for a second, shows where the game really governs the gear, and from then on that gear's top is the one learned there, kept in the gearbox's file (`Gauges\gearboxes\<name>.ini`, `Top1`, `Top2` ...), so the limiter and the floored points lie at the gear's real top. `Gauges.log` says each plateau it learns from;
- on a climb (the truck slowing with your foot on) it shifts down where floored would and holds the gear a few seconds after, so it does not shift up and straight back down;
- a kickdown is a push from part throttle: the pedal a fifth of the way down or more for a moment, then to the floor. A press from the foot off, as every press of a key is, kicks nothing down and drives by the floored shift points, which shift down where the truck is slow;
- such a push of the pedal to the floor kicks down at once to the gear that pulls the most, as far as the engine can take; wheelspin in mud and wheels locked under the brake do not shift it, as it weighs the wheels against the truck's speed over the ground; the first gear by the time the truck stops. With `Powertrain=1` the shift up out of first (or the gear it sets off in) goes by the wheels as they turn, so a truck spinning its wheels on dirt does not sit in first until the ground catches up; from the second gear up a shift up goes by the ground;
- `Input` (the Controls tab's "Driven with") fits the kickdown to what you drive with. A pad's trigger held near its end wavers, so on a pad the push has to come from under three quarters. The keyboard has no part throttle: no kickdown, and a key held shifts as the pedal floored;
- no shift down ever takes the engine near the red zone at the speed the wheels turn;
- the tachometer shows the converter's engine speed, and the cluster shows A. On a lift the needle follows the wheels down with the lockup held.

`Gearbox` has three levels, the three choices on the Gearbox tab:

- `0`: the game's own gearbox and automatic. The mod hooks nothing in the game's code and runs as 0.16.x did.
- `1`: the mod's automatic in A, as above. The game's shifter stays yours: a position picked there, L+ among them, hands the gearbox back until A is picked, which takes it again. So the automatic never shifts out of L+, and the game keeps the diff lock there. Only one thing of the game's is refused: the reverse its own automatic puts in while the truck stands in A with the brake down, which it can only do in the moment its automatic is up again (the shift modifier tapped, or A picked once more), so a truck in A no longer backs up on the brake; R picked on the game's shifter always passes. No bindings are needed.
- `2`: the mod's own gearbox takes the shifter from the moment a truck is driven, in whatever position the game had it, and refuses every shift of the game's. Driven with a pad or the keyboard (`Input` 1 or 2), the game's own shifter picks a position too: R, N, H, A, a numbered gear or the low range moved to there is read as that position's key, so a pad's or wheel's shifter keeps working beside the bindings. The shifter sends the position it stands at again each time its button is let go, so a position counts only when it differs from the last one the shifter sent: holding the shifter button for another control (the diff lock, a binding with that button in it) changes nothing. The cost: the position the shifter already stands at cannot be picked on it again. Its binding picks it, or move the shifter to another position first. With a wheel's pedals (`Input` 0) the game's shifts stay refused and the wheel's or shifter's buttons are the bindings; then it runs as `1` until a binding that can be pressed reaches reverse (Gear down, Reverse or Neutral: a key, a wheel's binding while that wheel is plugged in, a pad's while a pad is connected), so a truck can always be backed up. The game's L+ cannot be told from its first gear and reads as the first gear. While the NEXT GEN menu is open the gearbox stands where it is.

At `2` the positions are A (the automatic), the numbered gears, N, R, H and the low range L-, L and L+. Up and down walk the row R, N, L-, L, L+, 1, 2 and on to the top gear, as SMT's did. With `PositionsByGearbox` (Positions by gearbox, on by default) the row and the keys have only the positions the fitted gearbox has in the game: a gearbox with H and L only walks R, N, L, 1 and on, one with no low range R, N, 1 and on, and a key for a position it lacks does nothing and the log says so. A gearbox whose low range the game drives by hand keeps L- and L+. Off, every gearbox has all four. With `WalkSkipsLow` (Skip low range) the row is R, N, 1, 2 and on: up and down leave the low range but never enter it, so L-, L and L+ come only from their own keys, RangeLow or the game's shifter. L+ is the first gear at full power, so it and the 1st are the same gear. Up from the top gear stays there, and down from H lands on the top gear. Up from L+ goes to the 1st a second after an up press got there, so a quick run of presses up from R stops in L+. A 1st reached from the low range (by up, RangeHigh or the key 1) stays held until your next shift, so the low range never goes on to A by itself. On a pad RangeHigh has no button, so Gear up or A (Pad B) leaves it forward. Back from the map, the garage or the viewer the position stays while the game still has the gear and the power the mod set. A gear key sets its gear, and A hands the choice to the automatic until another position is picked. A or a gear picked from R while the truck still rolls back waits in N until the truck stands. In A a truck standing in N sets off in the first gear once the pedal goes down. The numbered gears, A and H keep the power the truck had when the gearbox took it, and the low range has power of its own. A range key moves between the low range and the gears for a shifter without a range of its own. `Gauges.log` says each kind of the game's shift it refused, once a truck.

Each action has two bindings, set on the Controls tab by a click and then a press. A control already held or a hat already pointing when you click is left alone until you let it go, and the next press binds. A binding is a key, a pad's buttons together, or a stick's direction with a button (LB and the right stick up gives `Pad LB+RSUp`), a wheel's or shifter's button or POV hat, or for the clutch a pedal's axis. A stick's direction counts once the stick is past half its travel; the eight are LSUp, LSDown, LSLeft, LSRight, RSUp, RSDown, RSLeft and RSRight. The game steers with the left stick and moves the camera with the right, so a direction bound alone fires while you steer or look around: bind it with a button held. Closing the menu ends a capture, and the menu's key, the cluster's toggle key and the PadToggle buttons are never bound. The controls are read about 200 times a second, so a quick tap still shifts. While the game's function menu is up, and for a quarter second after it closes, no binding acts and the game gets every press. A button held as the menu closes acts only once it is let go and pressed again. In `Gauges.ini` they are the `[Gearbox]` section's `Bind<Action>` and `Bind<Action>2` keys (`BindGearUp=Key Num 8`, `BindGear3=Wheel 2 Button 5`, `BindClutch2=Wheel 1 Axis 3 inverted`, `BindGearR=Pad LB+DPadDown`, `BindGearUp2=Pad LB+RSUp`, `None`), for Gear1 to Gear16, GearH, GearL-, GearL, GearL+, GearN, GearR, GearA, GearUp, GearDown, Clutch, RangeHigh and RangeLow, and DiffLock and Awd. The defaults are on the number pad, which the game, the ReShade the release brings and the usual mods leave alone: Num 8 for up, Num 2 for down, Num 5 for N, Num 0 for R and Num 7 for A. The first time the mod finds `SMT.ini` in `Sources\Bin` and no binding in its own ini, it takes over SMT's keyboard bindings (its diff lock and AWD keys too) and its four options once (its IMMERSIVE MODE as `DownStaysForward`) and says in the log what it took; SMT's pad and wheel section is left (its codes are not Windows'). `SMT.ini` itself is not changed.

The differential lock and all-wheel drive work on a key at every `Gearbox` level, `0` too, on every truck, the upgrade fitted or not. Their rows are under Locks and ride on the Controls tab, with no key by default. A press flips the truck's own switch, as SMT did, and the cluster's lamps show it. The game's own keys still work on a truck with the upgrade. Should the game switch a lock back off on a truck without the upgrade, `Gauges.log` says so once.

Five options, off by default:

- `RequireClutch=1`: a shift into a gear with the clutch not held, while the truck rolls at about 5 km/h or more, grinds and stays out: the gearbox goes to N and `Gauges.log` says so, as a running truck's gear does not go in unless the speeds match. Standing or crawling the gear is forced in and stalls the engine once. Into N nothing happens;
- `RequireGearHeld=1`: N the moment no gear key is held, for an H shifter with every gear bound (a gear picked on the game's shifter stays, as the shifter does);
- `SkipNeutral=1`: up and down step from the lowest forward place (L-, or 1 with `WalkSkipsLow`) to reverse and back without stopping in N;
- `DownStaysForward=1`: Gear down stops at the lowest forward place: the lowest of the low range the gearbox has, or 1 with `WalkSkipsLow` (the low range again once the truck is in it). A down press that would land on N or R does nothing, and N and R are on their own keys or the game's shifter, as SMT's immersive mode did. Up from R still walks N and on. With `SkipNeutral` on too, this one wins;
- `WalkSkipsLow=1`: Gear up and Gear down walk R, N, 1, 2 and on. L-, L and L+ come only from their own bindings, `RangeLow` or the game's shifter. Inside the low range up and down still step L-, L and L+, up from L+ goes on to 1 after its hold, and down from L- goes to N. A down press in D in the 1st, or standing in D, gives the 1st held, never N or R;
- `PositionsByGearbox=1` (the default): L-, L, L+ and H only where the fitted gearbox has them in the game, as its `GearboxParams` says. The mod reads them on the game's thread at each truck and each new fitted gearbox. A truck standing in a position its gearbox lacks goes to L, or to the 1st held, and H to the top gear. The game's shifter is never refused: where it gives a position the read lacks, the truck keeps every position until its gearbox changes. Without the read (a build where the mod does not find the game's lookup) every truck has all four;
- `IgnoreShifter=1`: a position picked on the game's shifter does nothing, with a pad, keys or a wheel, and only the bindings shift. The shifter still opens. Until a binding reaches R the mod's own gearbox runs as the automatic in A, as it does with a wheel's pedals.

`BackToA` makes Gearbox=2 a sequential box: shift up and down by hand, and some seconds after the last shift the gearbox goes back to A by itself. It waits until, for a whole second, the pedal is under 0.7, the wheels grip (no wheelspin, and not before the mod has learned their grip), the truck is not on a climb with the foot down or on a descent, it does not roll back, A's gear is the held one or the next (standing with the foot off, any), no diff lock or AWD switch was made in the last 3 s, the handbrake is off and no shift is under way. It never goes back from R, N, L-, L, L+ or H, with `RequireGearHeld` on, or from a gear picked on the game's shifter, as those stay where they were put. A shift by hand starts the time again. `Gauges.log` says what holds it, and when it goes back. The tab's Sequential button sets `RequireClutch` off, `RequireGearHeld` off and `BackToA` 10.

A stall from a shift without the clutch also costs the gearbox, with `Damage` on: `StallDamagePercent` of what it takes, 5 by default, 0 to 25 (the Gearbox tab's "Stall damage"). It is dealt the way Next Gen Tire Inflation deals tire damage: on the game's own thread, on its next frame, with the game's own damage update after it. So the game's truck card comes up, a garage repairs it, and the game does the rest. A stall the game causes by itself costs nothing. `Gauges.log` says what the gearbox took and the parts' damage the game summed after it.

The clutch burns too, with `Damage` on and `ClutchBurn=1` (the default; the Gearbox tab's "Clutch burns"), with a pedal or without. A clutch on a button, or none, works by itself and rides as a driver's foot would: it slips with the engine held up where the pedal asks until the truck has caught up, then locks. From 15 km/h up it does not slip at all: in too high a gear the engine lugs, and the mod raises the game's own stall seconds as the game does when it stalls an engine, so the game's "engine stalling" notice shows and the engine dies by the game's rule unless you shift down in time; the clutch heats only when crawling. A shift without the clutch under `RequireClutch`, standing or crawling, stalls the engine the same way, over a third of a second.

In a position of the mod's own gearbox (a numbered gear, N, R, H or the low range) there is one engine: the needle and the torque curve handed to the game go by the engine speed the clutch sets, as in A they go by the converter's. Its pedal is a torque, as a road truck's governor makes it: between the idle governor, which holds idle, and the high-speed one past the red zone. With the clutch on a pedal's axis, letting it up too fast or in too high a gear at a crawl stalls the engine, and the game's engine stalls with it. A clutch on a button, or none, works by itself and never stalls the engine as the truck moves off. Only a truck stuck in gear at idle stalls, whatever the clutch (Creep at idle below). A slipping clutch under load heats up by what it carries times the slip, scaled by the engine's own torque, and cools while it is locked or open. A box at the top left warns first, in the shape of Next Gen Tire Inflation's tire warning and right below it: amber "CLUTCH HOT" from half the burn point, red "CLUTCH DAMAGE" from the burn point, with the heat in percent of the burn point as a number and a bar. Only while it is red and the clutch still slips under load does the gearbox pay, about a twentieth of what it takes every two seconds. The moment the clutch grips or the throttle comes up the cost stops, however hot it still is, and it cools fast: the heat halves in about four seconds, so the box is amber again after about two and gone within about ten. It heats fully only at a crawl: under 5 km/h, a fifth as fast from 15 km/h up and in between in step, as a clutch slipping while the truck rolls is over in a moment and a truck pinned on the spot is the abuse. How fast it heats is the Gearbox tab's "Clutch heat" (`ClutchHeatPercent`, 60 by default; 100 is the model's own pace, lower gives more time to react; the cooling stays as it is). At the default 60 a floored start in third from a standstill goes amber after about three seconds and stays amber as the truck pulls away, and a truck that cannot pull away at all goes red after about six; at 100 the start in third goes amber after about two seconds and red after about four. A start in first never brings the box up, on a pedal or without one; an engine of three times the torque burns about three times as soon. A repair of the gearbox (the garage or a repair point) clears the heat, and nothing the burn owed lands on the repaired gearbox. Each kind of truck keeps its clutch's heat, so leaving it and coming back does not cool it at once. `Gauges.log` says when the burn begins, what it cost when it ends, and when a repair cleared the heat. With `Creep=1` a gear in with the clutch up moves the truck at idle, to about the gear's idle speed (Creep at idle below).

The mod sets its gears through the game's own shift function and puts the game's automatic flag down, so the game's shifter shows the gear's number and the cluster shows the gearbox's letter. The pull is still the game's: the truck drives with the game's torque in the gear the gearbox picks. `Gauges.log` says when the gearbox takes and lets go of a truck, each position it moves to and what moved it (the bindings, the game's shifter, the sequential, the gearbox damage or the gearbox's positions), and with `Probe=1` each shift and a line with the position, the clutch and the stalls.

`GameGearDisplay` is the game's own gear display at the bottom right. At 1, the default, it shows no text and no lit dot while the mod's own gearbox drives, since the cluster shows the gear. At 2 it is off always, and at 0 it is as the game has it. The shifter still opens on LB, and its dot shows while LB is held, so the stick can still pick a gear. The change comes at the display's next update: a gear change or an input. The mod changes the game's copy of two of the display's script functions in memory only. Where the game's code is not as the mod knows it, the display stays as it is and `Gauges.log` says why.

The manual transmission mod (SMT) is no longer needed and not compatible: the mod says so in the log and the tab when it finds it installed, and its gearbox stands down until SMT is removed.

## Creep at idle

By default the creep goes only where an automatic picks the gear (the author, 1 November): the mod's A (with the reverse and the range it drives there) or the game's own automatic. A gear picked by hand gets none, on either shifter and an R included: the game turns its automatic off to shift into R, so an R it took at a stop cannot be told from one picked by hand. With `CreepManual=1` (the Gearbox tab's "Creep in held gear", off by default) a gear picked by hand creeps too, with the governor, the holding part and the stall below. In Sequential the gearbox goes back to D before such a stall. While the torque cut drives a gear held by hand, the creep comes as the least pull of the cut instead of a throttle of its own.

With `Creep=1` (the default; the Gearbox tab's "Creep") an idling truck in gear moves as a real one does, and stands only on the brake or the handbrake. Through a torque converter (the game's automatic, the mod's A, and R, L- and L picked on the game's shifter at `Gearbox` 0 and 1) the truck creeps up to about walking pace, less as it rolls and nothing from 0.87 of the gear's idle speed: the speed the wheels turn at with the engine at idle, about a third of first gear's top on a truck. In R and the low range on the game's shifter the converter's push is smaller by the gear and comes in over half a second, so the truck creeps there without surging. The game's L+ is its first gear at full power, which the mod cannot tell from first, so it creeps as first gear does. In a gear picked by hand (the game's own, or a position of the mod's own gearbox with the clutch up) an idle governor holds the gear's idle speed, as a real diesel's does with whatever fuel it takes: less as the clutch is pressed and nothing with it down. It pulls away at idle only in the low gears. The governor comes in over about half a second, gives less in the low range and eases off before the wheels reach the idle speed, so in a low gear on soft ground the wheels and the needle stay steady. Once the truck rolls at more than 0.6 of the gear's idle speed it holds that speed in any gear, up to the engine's torque at idle: in 4th or 5th on a flat road the truck rolls on at idle with no pedal. When a climb or mud asks for more, the truck sags under the gear's idle speed. From a standstill in a tall gear the truck does not move off at idle. In the mod's own gearbox (`Gearbox=2`), in a position picked by hand, the engine then stalls as a manual one's does. Sagging, the needle (the clutch's engine there) follows it down, and under 0.6 of the idle speed with the governor at its most the engine stalls a second later. Stuck, with the wheels under a quarter of the gear's idle speed for a second once the game's pull has built, it stalls too. Hold the clutch, pick neutral or brake to keep it running. `Gauges.log` says "creep: the engine stalled, lugged under idle in gear N" or "stuck in gear N". No stall comes on the automatic (`Gearbox=0` or `1`, R and the low range on the game's shifter too), in A or within a second of a shift: there the governor pushes and the engine idles on. Downhill over the gear's idle speed the governor gives nothing and the engine brakes. In A the converter takes it and the engine never stalls. The mod raises only the throttle the game hands its wheels, for the truck you drive: the pedal, the game's engine speed, its fuel and its sound stay at idle, and the needle shows idle except while the engine lugs in the mod's own gearbox. A light foot never drops the creep: the wheels get the larger of the two. Nothing in neutral, with the engine off or starting, in a menu or the pause, or for any other truck. How strong the creep is (0.15 of the throttle at a standstill) is a first figure: `Gauges.log` has a line as each creep begins and ends, and with `Probe=1` four a second. On a build where the mod does not find the game's call of its wheels' drive the truck stands at idle as in the game, with a line in `Gauges.log`.

## The mod's engine

The game's engine gives all of its torque with the wheels at about half of the gear's top speed, and less and less from there to nothing at the top, whatever the engine speed. The mod learns each gear's top where the game's pull reaches nothing floored on the flat, for each truck kind as a share of the game's gear speed, kept in the truck's file. A top found far off the one kept replaces it. `Powertrain=1` (the Power tab's "Real engine") hands the game the mod's engine in its place: the torque curve of the truck's class against the engine speed the needle shows, and with the mod's automatic doubled by the converter at its stall, fading out as the truck catches up with the engine. Cold oil (up to 15 percent at 0 C) and a turbo still spooling (the torque the air allows: about 0.43 of it at the first instant on a 1.8 bar truck, all of it once the boost has built) only cost torque where the pedal asks for more than they leave: at a light foot the engine gives what the pedal asks. Wheels spinning in mud keep the game's own fall toward the gear's top, which holds them back, as the lift goes by the truck's speed over the ground. So a gear revved high pulls as a real engine's would, to about 0.92 of the gear's top speed and from there less, to nothing at the top, as a diesel's governor cuts the fuel; pulling away and a gear too tall on a climb get the converter's push. A gear's top speed stays the game's: at it and past it the game gets its own factor back. In a gear of the mod gearbox held by hand it is different, see below.

At road speed the game moves the truck partly by a push on the chassis, sized for its own torque. With `Powertrain=1` that push carries no more than the pull of the mod engine and never less than the game gives. So a step in the pull, or wheels that catch grip, no longer shoot the truck forward, and the mod engine shows at every speed. `PushShare` (0 to 100, 0 by default, no box) gives that share of the old push back: 100 is as before.

The game's diff lock is a rule on the torque, not a coupling: a driven wheel that runs ahead of the slowest loses its torque, and nothing ties the wheels' speeds. So a free wheel hunts, a wheel in the air still spins up, and with the foot off the lock does nothing. With `Powertrain=1` and the diff lock on, Diff lock bite on the Power tab (`DiffLockBite`, 0 to 100, 0 by default) moves each driven wheel's spin toward the driven wheels' mean after the game's drive, so they turn together, at every `Gearbox` level. It adds no drive and no brake of its own. While the engine pulls it writes no wheel faster than the fastest the drive left, turns none backward and adds no spin in sum. 0 leaves the game's lock as it is, and 100 turns the driven wheels as one shaft. A locked truck then scrubs in a turn on hard ground, as a real one does. Nothing is written while the handbrake is on, unless the truck's Diff is Locked. A truck whose lock is always on gets the bite too, and then scrubs in every turn on hard ground. `Gauges.log` says when the bite acts on a truck and when it rests for its kind, and with `Probe=1` a line a second gives the figures. On a build where the mod does not find the game's diff lock setter the bite is off, with a line in the log.

Diff on the Power tab (`DiffType`) is the differential of the driven truck, and each truck keeps its own in its file. It acts with `Powertrain=1` in the gear positions of the mod's gearbox, not in A. Open acts in each axle by itself: an open differential gives the two wheels of its axle the same torque. A free wheel spins and takes the pull from the other wheel of its axle. The other axles take over what the free wheel cannot use, as the shaft between the axles is tied: in L-, L, L+ and R the drive that the wheel cap holds back on the axle of the free wheel goes to the other axles, and they pull on up to the wheel cap. In a numbered gear nothing is held back, so nothing is handed over. A truck with a free wheel on every axle still wants the diff lock or Limited slip. In L-, L, L+ and R the mean of each axle's two wheels is held at the ceiling of the wheel cap. With every wheel on the ground each wheel stays at that ceiling, as on the other types. A free wheel with its partner standing can run to twice that ceiling. In a numbered gear only the engine's curve holds it. Two wheels are one axle when they sit on the same part of the frame, on its two sides and opposite each other. A wheel without such a partner is an axle of its own. Limited slip is the mod as it was before the types: it holds a spinning wheel in L-, L, L+ and R. Locked keeps the diff lock on. It holds a free wheel only with Diff lock bite above 0, and at 0 it is the game's lock. Auto picks Locked where the truck's lock is always on, else Open, and Limited slip where the mod cannot read the truck's lock. With the game's diff lock switched on, the lock acts on every type, so on a truck whose lock is always on Open and Limited slip may change nothing. With no truck driven the row sets `DiffType` in `Gauges.ini`, which a truck without its own takes.

`EngineShare` sets how much of the mod's engine the game gets: at 50, the default, the truck pulls half way between the game's own engine and the mod's, and at 100 all of the mod's. Lower it where a truck pulls too hard.

`RoadLoad=1` (the Power tab's "Air drag", on by default; `RoadLoad=0` turns it off) takes the air's drag off the engine's pull: ½ × 1.2 × CdA × v² over the truck's weight, about 0.5 kN at 100 km/h for a pickup. It goes against each gear's own pull at the game's torque, which the mod learns when you drive that gear floored for a couple of seconds in all, over as many visits as it takes, with the wheels up to 0.8 of the gear's top and on grades up to 3° (kept as `Base1.<engine>` ... in the truck's file, so an engine upgrade starts over); until then that gear has none, unless two learned gears agree within 15 % (their pull times their top): then it goes by them. That pull is learned with the wheels' own drag left out, so Rolling drag and a flat tire don't make Air drag stronger. Figures learned before that are dropped once and learned again (`BaseDamp.<engine>=1` marks the new ones). CdA goes by the truck's weight, 1.1 m² under 4 t, 3.5 under 10 t, else 6, unless `RoadCdA` sets it. The weight is the truck's own, without cargo or a trailer, so with a load the drag taken off is too much. The game already slows a rolling truck by itself, more the faster it goes. Air drag adds the air's share on top of that. The game has its own rolling resistance.

In a gear of the mod gearbox held by hand (`Gearbox=2`) `Powertrain=1` with `ModEngineSpeed=1` goes further: the truck pulls with the mod engine alone, and the game's own fall toward the gear's top is gone. The game cut its pull at a gear's top and gave it back only at about 0.55 of it, so floored in L- the truck climbed to the top, sagged and climbed again every few seconds, with the game's own gearbox too. The mod now answers the game's call for the gear's torque from its wheels' drive: the mod engine's curve at the engine speed the needle shows, with the cold oil, the boost and the air drag as above. With the clutch slipping it is the torque through the clutch. Floored in L- the needle climbs to the redline and holds. In deep mud a wheel that breaks loose no longer runs away: the game's own brake on a wheel past the gear's top acts again, and after a cut the pull comes back at the pace of the engine's response, as in the game. So in deep mud the low range pulls a little softer. In a gear held by hand the governor goes by the speed of the truck itself, not by the wheels, so the engine holds the truck at the top speed of the gear as a governor does, and a wheel that spins or hops no longer makes the pull jump. In L-, L, L+ and R no driven wheel can run more than half again past that top: in mud or with a wheel in the air the wheels stop there and the needle stays steady. `WheelCap` (150 by default, 110 to 200, no box) sets that limit in percent of the top. Near the top of the gear the limit comes down with the speed of the truck: from 0.75 of the top it falls smoothly, and with the truck at 1.1 of the top it is 1.1 of the top. This is now the governor of the low range and of R. So between 0.75 and 1.1 of the top of a low gear the truck pulls less than before. That pull is what swayed: floored in L- the truck and the needle swung up and down around the top. Now the truck settles near the top, under it with a heavy load, and the needle holds. The limit takes away only what the engine adds in a step. A wheel that the ground or a slope turns faster is left alone. Where the wheels are held there, the burn follows what they keep. In the numbered gears a governor takes the pull off from the redline, to nothing at 1.1 of it where the needle stops. The pedal is the fuel, as the game has it: half way down gives half the pull, as before. `EngineShare` mixes the game's curve back in: at 100 the mod engine pulls alone, at 0 the game's own drives. With the foot off the engine brakes the truck. `EngineBrake` (the Power tab's "Engine braking", 0 to 30, 15 by default) is the percent of the gear's torque it holds back at the redline. It brakes less toward idle and not at all at idle, so a standing truck is never pushed, and rolling back against a forward gear it does not brake. It brakes only while every driven wheel turns with the gear, so a slow or stopped wheel is never spun backwards. The needle follows the driven wheels with their signs, so one wheel spinning the wrong way no longer pins it at its stop. Right after a shift the game's own torque drives until the game's gear agrees with the position. The fuel follows the mod engine's pull. So floored near a gear's top the truck burns what it pulls, where the game burned near idle because it pulled nothing there. In the middle of a gear the burn is as before. The range figure follows the burn. While the mod engine pulls alone the gear's top and Air drag's pull are not learned, as there is no game pull to learn from. When the game's damage throws the gearbox out, the mod gearbox goes to N too, with a line in `Gauges.log`, with `Powertrain` on or off. It needs `ModEngineSpeed=1`, as the game's own engine speed would read a floored pedal: with it off a held gear pulls through the game's torque factor as before. The game's other calls of the gear torque, the stall's and the input's, keep the game's own. `Gauges.log` names the function and the drive's call at the first truck and says when the mod engine takes over and why not. With `Probe=1` a line a second gives the figures. On a build where the mod does not find them the game's torque stays its own, with a line in the log. `CrawlHold=` is no longer read and goes at the next write.

`ModEngineSpeed=1` (the Power tab's "Mod engine speed", on by default, with `Powertrain=1`) hands the game the mod's engine speed. The game has no engine speed of its own to read: it works one out on each call, 0 to 1, from the pedal, its own pull and the wheels, and 0 with the foot off. So the cockpit's needles sat at idle while the mod's needle showed the engine turning. A hook on that function now answers with the mod's needle while the mod drives the truck: 0 at idle, 1 at the redline times `EngineSpeedTop`. The cockpit's needles, the steering wheel's rev lights, the engine sound, the effects and the exhaust follow it. The engine sound of the game goes partly by the engine speed and partly by how long the pedal has been held in the gear: the engine speed gives about two thirds of the rise of the sound in 1st, R and N, 40 percent in 2nd and a quarter from 3rd up. So from 3rd up the sound moves less than the needle, and keeps rising for a moment after the needle holds. Fuel use and the stall keep the game rules, and in a gear held by hand they follow the pull of the mod engine. Off, with `Powertrain=0`, for another truck, or with the engine off, the game computes its own. With `Probe=1` the log has a line a second with the game's own value for each of its callers beside the mod's.

With the engine handed over, the mod's automatic shifts as a truck automatic does:

- floored, it takes each gear to the red zone, or shifts up sooner where the next gear already pulls harder, by the engine's power (a profile that still gains power up to the red zone, as one built from a peak does, is revved near the top): the game's pull falls away toward a gear's top whatever the engine, so at a lower `EngineShare` the truck would otherwise hang short of the red zone; at a lighter foot it shifts up about as far along as the pedal is down: a fifth of the pedal, a fifth of the way from idle to there (a diesel at about 1500 rpm, as a road car's automatic shifts), but never under 0.8 of where the engine's curve first reaches nine tenths of its torque, and no later than 0.45 of the red zone (a scout without a profile cruises from about 2370 rpm), where it begins to pull (see the engine profiles below);
- it shifts down where the gear below would come in clear of its own point up (15 percent under it), whatever the gearbox's steps, so it does not shift up and straight back down; at a light foot not under 1.6 times its idle (about 1040 rpm on a truck's scales), where a diesel still pulls cleanly, so a gentle shift up waits until it lands over that;
- the converter locks up from 1.65 times idle at a light foot (about 1070 rpm on a truck), so a gentle shift up lands locked, and from half the red zone floored, from the first gear on; locked, it lets go with the foot on only under 1.4 times idle and on a coast under 1.04, whatever the pedal does;
- one gear up at a time; going up through the gears, from a standstill or after a shift up, each gear is held at least two and a half seconds of moving, but on the limiter only 0.9 s, so a light truck that reaches a gear's top quickly does not sit on the governor (a scout one and a half, by the truck's class: the class of its own scales, and for a truck without them the class of its weight, a scout up to 6 tonnes, a truck under 16, a heavy truck from there; the Gearbox tab's slider "Upshift delay", 1 to 3 seconds, sets it for the gearbox fitted, kept in its file `Gauges\gearboxes\<name>.ini` as `GearHold`), so a light truck does not run through its gears in a moment; a gear a kickdown brings is not held; a kickdown goes to the lowest gear that keeps the engine under the red zone and short of where it would shift straight back up;
- a game's gearbox is set up for the game's own engine, with a first gear for crawling, so the automatic sets off in the lowest gear with at least a seventh of the top gear's speed, and lower where the engine is weak for the truck: its torque over the truck's mass, both read from the game's files (a scout of 9 tonnes sets off in second with an engine of 195000, in first with one of 100000). It takes the gears under that only when the truck is stuck: floored, slow and not gaining for a second. The mass is the truck's own, without addons, cargo or a trailer, so a loaded truck that cannot pull away in its start gear takes the first by the stuck rule;
- a truck losing speed with the pedal under 0.4 is not taken for one climbing, so a light foot does not bring the floored points and an early shift down;
- a shift up the next gear cannot hold, up a grade or through mud, that comes back down within six seconds with the foot on keeps the gear below: no shift up past it for eight seconds, on the limiter if it comes to that, as a loaded truck sits at its governed speed on a hill, then one more try; each try taken back doubles the wait, up to 30 seconds. Lifting the foot, a stop or a kickdown lets it go, and so does a try that holds. The log says each time.

Each engine can have a profile of its own, in its own file `Gauges\engines\<name>.ini` (the name the game's files give it, which the tab and `Gauges.log` show). The tab's Engine section sets it while the truck is driven, and a plot there shows the torque curve in force:

- `PeakTorqueRpm`: the engine speed of its peak torque, as its maker gives it (most engines' figures are published). The torque curve is built around it: a turbo diesel's broad plateau, from 0.85 to 1.5 times that speed, with three fifths of the torque at half of it and four fifths left at the red zone; a naturally aspirated engine's narrower hump around the peak. Without it the engine pulls by its class's curve;
- `Aspiration`: 0 turbo, 1 naturally aspirated. Naturally aspirated has no boost, so no boost dial and no turbo sounds, and all of its torque at once with the pedal;
- `Fuel`: `diesel` or `petrol`, in place of the class's (trucks and heavy trucks diesel, scouts petrol). A diesel's boost goes with its fuel. A petrol engine's throttle holds its manifold under the atmosphere at idle and a light foot. A naturally aspirated engine has no boost whatever its fuel;
- `TorqueCurve`: a whole curve of its own, written by hand as `x:y` points (`0.3:0.7,0.5:1,1:0.75`, x the engine speed over the red zone's, y the torque over its most), in place of the one from the peak;
- `Torque`: how much of its torque the truck gets with the engine handed to the game, in percent (10 to 100; 100 when not set). The Engine tab's "Engine torque" slider. Lower it for an RLM-like engine with several times a stock engine's torque: at 25 an engine with four times the torque pulls like the stock one. The set-off gear follows it. A truck's own `Torque.<engine>` in its file comes first.

```
[Engine]
Name=us_truck_old_engine_0
PeakTorqueRpm=1400
Aspiration=0
```

The scales (idle, the red zone, the tachometer's top) stay the truck's. Each engine's profile goes with the engine, so a truck fitted with another engine in the garage pulls by that one's. A file written or changed by hand counts from the tab's reload button, or the next time the truck is driven.

This file is the engine's default. A kind of truck, or one truck, can keep a profile of its own for that engine in its file in `Gauges\trucks`, as Engine torque is kept (see the scales above). Two kinds with the same engine then pull by their own curves. With no profile set for the truck or its kind, the first change on the tab makes the kind's own, and the engine's file stays as it is. A change while the truck's set is kept with `Own=0` edits this file. The line In force under Fuel names the level in force. Class curve gives the class's curve at that level: for a truck or a kind it writes `PeakTorqueRpm.<engine>=0`, and here it takes the four keys out and keeps `Torque`. Make this the engine's default copies a truck's profile here whole. Defaults leaves the profile here as it is. One truck's set goes by the game's id, which can change at a load, so that truck can lose its set until the mod gives trucks an id of its own. The kind's set keeps.

The mod does it through the game's torque factor, the share of its torque the game leaves a damaged engine: it writes the factor on every pass, at most six times the game's own for a truck or a heavy truck and three times for a scout (by the class the automatic goes by), and over a gear's last three tenths that roof falls in a straight line to the game's own at the gear's top, so the push dies down before each shift (a vanilla Defender shot from 27 to 58 km/h in 5 seconds through three gears, 27 October). Within a gear a push that grows after a cut (within two seconds of it) comes back at a set pace, about 1 a second, so a truck held near a gear's top settles instead of losing and regaining its push; an ordinary rise through a gear, a push that falls, a new gear and the first write come at once. While the turbo spools the engine has the torque the air allows, and all of its curve once the boost has built (review 12). The factor goes times the engine's `Torque` share where one is set, and the mod gives the game its own back when the setting goes off, the engine stops, a pull test is armed or another truck is driven. The game's own share stays in it: a damaged engine pulls less, as before. The probe line "powertrain" says what is written and the factor's own terms (the wheels' share of the gear's top it went by, the roof there, whether the rise was paced); its power and fuel are the model's own, by the shaped pedal, which the game does not use. The line "automatic" ends with what its rules decide by: the pedal they go by, the points up and down, the converter's lock and unlock points, the pause and the hold, the kickdown's arm, the timers and the start gear.

The Power tab has a pull test, for the engine's torque the mod will hand to the game next. Press its Start button, then on a long flat road drive in the fourth gear or higher and floor the pedal for seven seconds. The mod holds the gear, changes the game's torque factor (what it leaves a damaged engine) for a second at a time (halved, raised by half, at three quarters) and measures how hard the truck pulls, then puts the factor back. Lifting the foot, braking or another gear stops it. The numbers go into `Gauges.log`.

## Physics rate, a test

`PhysicsRate=120` (Mod work, Test: Physics rate) steps the physics of the game 120 times a second in place of 60. It is a test and is off by default. It is for single player only: nothing checks that every player in co-op runs the same rate. The key is read once as the game starts, so a change on the tab is saved and runs after a restart. The row says which rate is running.

At 120 the mod halves what the drive adds to the wheels each step, so a truck pulls and revs as it does at 60. Not corrected yet: the brakes, the forces of water, mud and snow, and some smoothing of the wheels. Expect those to feel different. The physics costs more processor time, most at a low frame rate. If one place in the game code is not found, nothing is changed and `Gauges.log` says which.

`PhysicsIterations` (ini only, with 120) sets the solver iterations a step: 0 is the mod choice (2), or 2 or 4. At 4 the suspension is not corrected and is likely far stiffer. It is there for a measurement.

The idea of a higher physics rate, and the first map of what has to be scaled, come from True 240Hz Physics Patch by alounana on mod.io.

## With Next Gen Tire Inflation

Next Gen Tire Inflation (the SnowRunner Tire Inflation System) and this mod are made to run side by side, each with its own ini and log, their tabs in the one NEXT GEN menu. The same `version.dll` loads both `.asi` files. This mod does not touch the pad's buttons, so that mod's panel keeps working as before.

The mod publishes its state for other mods to read: a block of 256 bytes of shared memory named `Local\SnowRunnerGauges.State.<process id>`, written about 20 times a second. It holds the truck driven, the outside temperature in force, and everything the cluster shows as it shows it: the speed, the gear and its letter, the dials' scales, the temperatures, boost, air, fuel, range, the lamps and the units the player picked. A speedo of another mod can draw from it and agree with the cluster. A tire mod by the same author reads the outside temperature from it. `docs\SnowRunnerGauges_State.h` has the layout and the read rule, with an example reader. It needs only `windows.h` and `stdint.h`, and the mod builds its writer on the same file. Version 1 was the first 64 bytes, and readers of it still work.

From the version of that mod that publishes its own state, the cluster has a tenth lamp: a tire with the letter of the pressure mode you chose there, L for Low, R for Reduced, N for Normal, + for Increased. It is lit amber at Low and Reduced and green at Increased, dark at Normal, it blinks while the pressure is on its way, and it is red while the tires take damage or one is worn out. Without that mod, or with an older version of it, there are nine lamps as before.

## Remove

Delete `Gauges.asi`, `Gauges.ini`, `Gauges.log` and the folder `Gauges` (the setups of trucks, gearboxes and engines, and the older logs) from `Sources\Bin`. `NextGen.ini` and `version.dll` go too when no other Next Gen mod is left.

## Notes

- The mod changes no game file. What it writes into the game's memory: with `Damage` on, the engine damage of the truck you drive, and its gearbox damage on a stall from a shift without the clutch or from a burning clutch (on the game's thread, through a hook on its truck update, with the game's damage update after); with `Gearbox=1`, the game's automatic flag, the gears through the game's own shift function, the power factor of the low range, and the stall seconds for a shift without the clutch under `RequireClutch`; with `Powertrain=1`, the game's torque factor of the truck you drive, on every pass; during the pull test, the torque factor, for seven seconds. With all of them off it writes nothing. The hooks in the game's code go by the level of `Gearbox`: at `0` none at all; at `1` and `2` the eight gearbox functions, calling through; at `2` with `Damage` on also the game's truck update. Each is put in the first time the level needs it, also when the tab turns it on, and stays until the game is started again.
- `Damage` has not been tried in co-op. Leave it off there.
- On a PC whose Windows proxy settings are on automatic detection the first frame can come late by half a minute: ReShade checks for its own update at the start and waits on that lookup.
- When another program holds the game's swap chain in a way the mod cannot draw on, `Gauges.log` names that program.
- If something does not work, `Gauges.log` in `Sources\Bin` says what the mod found and did. `GrabDumps.bat` from the zip, run in `Sources\Bin` with the game closed, gathers the logs, the ini files and the crash dump into one zip beside itself, for a report. It is the log of the game's last start; at each start the one before moves into `Gauges\logs`, named by the date and time that start began (`Gauges 2026-10-12 21-29-11.log`). The folder keeps the newest 20 of them and at most 500 MB (`KeepLogs`, 0 keeps all); older ones go at the next start, and only the mod's own date-named logs are ever removed. With `Probe=1` a long drive's log runs to 20 or 30 MB. The log stays open while the game runs and its probe lines go out every half second; every other line is on disk at once.
- The game's own HUD stays as it is. Its "Immersive Mode" option hides it while it is not in use.

## Build

Windows with Visual Studio 2022 (C++ desktop tools).

```
build.bat
test.bat
```

`build.bat` makes `out\Gauges.asi`, the four offline test programs in `out\test`, and `out\version.dll`, the loader, from the sources in `tools`. `test.bat` runs the tests: every setting through the ini and back, the trucks' own scales, the reader of the game's memory and the write of engine damage against a made up game, the engine speed, the range, the tilt, the coolant and oil temperatures, the oil warning, the engine damage and the turbo's rotor and boost on made up drives and in the closed loop, and a preview that draws the cluster and the settings tab into PNG files in `out\preview` without the game. With `SR_IMAGE` set to a copy of the game exe's image from a running game, the truck test also looks for the game's places and its shift function in it, and checks them against the builds it knows (the Steam build of 22 July 2026 and the update of 20 October). The preview needs the full Dear ImGui sources in `deps\imgui_src`, see `test\build_preview.bat`. Without them the rest is still built. `test\build_menu_preview.bat` makes `out\test\menu_preview.exe`, which hooks the mod's overlay onto a swap chain of its own without the game and draws the NEXT GEN menu into PNG files.

What the mod is built with is in `deps`, each with its licence: Dear ImGui, compiled in; the ReShade add-on API's headers, used to be handed the game's swap chains by ReShade (Next Gen Tire Inflation's `tools/reshade_nobanner.py` and `tools/ReShade.ini` make the ReShade the release brings); and stb_truetype with stb_rect_pack.

## How it works

A thread in the game process reads the driven truck's values from the game's memory 20 times a second, works out the engine speed, the range, the tilt, the boost and the coolant and oil temperatures from them, and keeps the settings. With `Damage` on, the same thread writes the engine damage that the temperature caused into the game, at most once a second and only while a truck is driven. The mod hooks the game's Present, the call that puts each frame on the screen, and draws the cluster and the menu with Dear ImGui on the game's own device just before the frame goes out. It takes the game's imports of `CreateDXGIFactory1` and `D3D11CreateDevice` and gives each swap chain the game makes a method table of its own with the mod's Present (and Present1). Whatever was in the table before (DirectX, the Steam overlay's hook on it, another mod) is called after the mod has drawn. DirectX's own method tables stay as they are: the Steam overlay hooks whatever they hold each time a swap chain is made, and with the mod's hook in them the two called each other until the game ran out of stack. Where ReShade is installed, ReShade hands the mod each swap chain the game makes at its presents instead, as anything the mod made at start would run into ReShade setting up its hooks. The release brings ReShade 6.8.0 without its start banner, with a `ReShade.ini` that loads no effects and keeps Home for ReShade's own window: on a tester's PC with RivaTuner, Discord's overlay and two monitors the game crashed without ReShade and not with it. Where ReShade is missing or does not take the mod as an add-on, the start says so in the log, in a line at the top for 15 seconds and in the NEXT GEN menu. The text is baked from the game's own font files when the game starts, at the size the cluster has on your screen.

The sounds are played through XAudio2 2.9, the copy the game brings or Windows' own, on a thread of their own. The mod takes the audio device when a sound is due and lets go of it a few seconds after the last one.
